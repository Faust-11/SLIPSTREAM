## Sunday in the Ardennes

The 2021 Belgian Grand Prix, round 12 of the season, was due to take place on 29 August at Spa-Francorchamps.[^c1] The grid promised an interesting race: Max Verstappen started from pole, George Russell's Williams was a surprise alongside him, and Lewis Hamilton was only third.[^c2]

The start was scheduled for 15:00 local time.[^c3] Half an hour before that, on his way to the grid, Sergio Pérez lost control of his Red Bull at Les Combes and hit the barrier.[^c4] The rain did not ease. The start was delayed by 10 minutes, then by another 15, and only at 15:25 did the safety car lead the drivers out onto the track.[^c5]

Opinions in the cockpits differed. Pole-sitter Verstappen, with only the safety car ahead of him, said: "I think it's wet, but for me it's fine to race." Charles Leclerc saw a very different picture: "We have absolutely no visibility."[^c6]

On the second lap behind the safety car the start procedure was suspended, and race control showed the red flag before the race had officially begun.[^c7] The cars returned to the pits. The wait began. Red Bull used the break to repair Pérez's car, and Masi allowed him to start from the pit lane.[^c8]

## The clock that was stopped

The regulations give a race three hours. The countdown started at 15:00, the scheduled start time, not when the cars went out on track, so everything had to be over by 18:00.[^c9]

At 17:00 the stewards took a rare step. Citing the FIA International Sporting Code, they temporarily stopped the race "on the grounds of force majeure". The clock stopped along with the race: once it resumed, one hour of racing would remain.[^c10]

Race director Michael Masi rejected the idea of moving the race to Monday: "There is no ability to postpone the race until tomorrow."[^c11] Formula 1 chief executive Stefano Domenicali explained why: the marshals are volunteers, and too many of them would have to be at work on Monday.[^c12]

## A few laps behind the safety car

At 18:17 the cars left the pit lane again behind the safety car.[^c13] Outlets count the laps they covered differently. Formula 1 and The Race write of three laps, Speedcafe and Autosport of two. RaceFans breaks it down like this: before the first red flag there was a formation lap and one more lap, and after the restart there were two laps. The FIA classification states that the leader crossed the control line three times.[^c14] Then came another red flag, and at 18:45 it was announced that the race would not resume.[^c15]

Masi insisted the cars had not been sent out just for the record books: "It was to see what the conditions are like." According to him, the forecasters saw a window in the weather, but "then the weather came in again and got the better of us".[^c16]

## How the result was calculated

The FIA classification explains the arithmetic. The result was taken at the end of the first lap: under Article 51.14 of the sporting regulations, the classification is fixed at the penultimate lap before the lap during which the signal to suspend the race was given. For points, Article 6.5 applied. The leader had crossed the control line three times, meaning he had completed more than two laps, so half points could be awarded.[^c17] The rule at the time was simple: if the leader had completed two laps or less, no points; if less than 75% of the distance, half points.[^c18]

Officially the race lasted one lap of 6.880 km, and Verstappen "won" it in 3:27.071.[^c19] A full lap of Spa is 7.004 km. The 124-metre difference comes from the fact that the start and finish lines at Spa are not in the same place.[^c20]

Verstappen received 12.5 points, Russell 9, Hamilton 7.5. The whole top ten got half points.[^c21] For Russell it was his first Formula 1 podium.[^c22] Pérez, who never started from the grid, finished 19th. Lance Stroll received a 10-second penalty because his car was modified during the race stoppage, and dropped to 20th.[^c23]

## A record nobody is proud of

Spa 2021 became the shortest race in world championship history. The previous record belonged to the 1991 Australian Grand Prix: that race was stopped after 14 laps and never resumed.[^c24] By RaceFans' count, half points had been awarded only five times before, and Spa was the sixth case in 1,047 rounds.[^c25]

The winner did not pretend to celebrate. "Of course, it's a win but it's not how you want to win," said Verstappen.[^c26]

## "Money talks"

Hamilton was far blunter. "Money talks," he said, adding: "I think the sport made a bad choice today."[^c27] In the evening he wrote: "Today was a farce and the only people to lose out are the fans who have paid good money to watch us race."[^c28] He believed the spectators should get their money back, and if not, be given a free race: "I'd absolutely come back."[^c29]

Domenicali rejected the accusations: "It had nothing to do with money or politics. We get the entry fee regardless of whether the grand prix is driven or not."[^c30]

## Compensation without money

Spectators stood in the rain for more than three hours.[^c31] They never got a refund. In December 2021 the promoter, Spa Grand Prix, wrote to ticket holders that the cost of their tickets would not be refunded.[^c32] Instead it offered a prize draw for 170 tickets to the 2022 race (100 seats in silver grandstands, 50 in gold and 20 VIP), an invitation to a special event on the Thursday before the next Grand Prix and one F1 TV pass per ticket.[^c33] The offer had to be taken up by the end of January 2022.[^c34]

One angry fan told the broadcaster RTL: "I paid €900 for a mockery of a Grand Prix, and I'm being offered an entry into a prize draw. Are they kidding?"[^c35]

## New rules

On 14 February 2022 the F1 Commission approved changes to the sporting regulations; they still had to be ratified by the FIA World Motor Sport Council.[^c36] From now on no points are awarded unless the leader has completed at least two laps without a safety car or virtual safety car.[^c37] Then come the steps. If the leader has completed more than two laps but less than 25% of the distance, the top five score on a 6-4-3-2-1 scale. From 25% to 50% the top nine score (13-10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1), from 50% to 75% the top ten (19-14-12-9-8-6-5-3-2-1).[^c38] Under these rules the 2021 Belgian Grand Prix would not have produced a single point.[^c39]

The new rules were tested that very autumn. The 2022 Japanese Grand Prix was stopped after two laps because of rain and resumed more than two hours later. Of the 53 scheduled laps, 28 were completed.[^c40] Verstappen received the full 25 points, and that was enough for the title.[^c41] The FIA explained that reduced points applied only to races that were suspended and not resumed, and the race at Suzuka had been resumed.[^c42]

On 21 February 2023 the F1 Commission approved new wording "to ensure that shorter races have reduced points even if they don't finish with a suspended race".[^c43]
