## A wet morning

The 1996 Monaco Grand Prix, round six of the season, was held on 19 May.[^c1] On Sunday morning a downpour swept over the principality, and by the start the track was beginning to dry. Everyone started on wet tyres except Jos Verstappen, who gambled on slicks.[^c2]

Michael Schumacher took pole for Ferrari, Damon Hill lined up alongside him and Jean Alesi was third.[^c3] Olivier Panis, in the Mugen-Honda-powered Ligier JS43, started only 14th.[^c38][^c4] An electrical fault had ruined his qualifying,[^c5] and the mood in the garage matched it:

> "My engineers were upset — one of the Japanese guys was even crying."[^c6]

Panis set about cheering the team up himself and promised they would score points on Sunday. He later admitted he said it mostly for morale.[^c37] But in the Sunday warm-up it was he who set the fastest time.[^c36] According to GrandPrix.com, already in qualifying, before his engine trouble, Panis reckoned his car was really quick and capable of a top-six place.[^c52]

The sources do not even agree on how many cars started. A wire report from that week and some later pieces speak of 22 starters. But Andrea Montermini wrecked his Forti coming out of the tunnel and, according to GrandPrix.com's results, did not take the start, so 21 cars began the race.[^c7]

## The first lap

Schumacher went off on the very first lap.[^c8] His explanation came quickly:

> "It was a stupidity. It was a driving mistake, I am also capable of making mistakes."[^c9]

On the same lap the race ended for Rubens Barrichello, Verstappen and both Minardi drivers, Pedro Lamy and Giancarlo Fisichella.[^c10] Giancarlo Minardi, whose team had lost both cars in a single lap, did not hold back:

> "In 12 years of F1 I have never seen anything like this."[^c39]

Panis's team-mate Pedro Diniz lasted only five laps before his transmission failed.[^c48]

Hill took the lead and by lap 21 was almost 25 seconds clear.[^c11] It looked as though Williams would cruise to victory. But after 40 laps, just past half-distance, Hill's engine failed.[^c12]

> "I thought I had it. I had a good grip on the race, enough fuel to finish and a good gap on Jean Alesi."[^c13]

## Betting on a full tank

Panis, meanwhile, was running his own race. Against his engineers' advice he had asked for the tank to be filled to the brim so he could run the first, wet stint for as long as possible without a stop.[^c14] At the stop itself the mechanics added only a splash, and after the race Panis did not even know he had been refuelled.[^c47] At the Loews hairpin he dived down the inside to pass Eddie Irvine for third, and the cars touched.[^c15] Soon afterwards he nearly lost everything on the oil left by Hill's engine:

[[media:m2]]

> "I did a 360-degree spin, I hit nothing, and I got going again."[^c16]

Alesi was the next victim. On lap 54 he pitted for a check and rejoined,[^c49] but after 60 laps the suspension on his Benetton broke, and Panis took the lead.[^c17] Behind him the carnage continued. As early as lap 18 Heinz-Harald Frentzen had run into the back of Irvine's Ferrari at Sainte Dévote and pitted for a new nose.[^c41] Jacques Villeneuve was taken out by Luca Badoer.[^c18] Near the end Irvine spun, and Mika Salo and Mika Häkkinen ran into his Ferrari.[^c19]

## The two-hour limit

The race never ran its full distance. The two-hour limit kicked in, and instead of the scheduled 78 laps the chequered flag came out after the 75th.[^c20] Panis covered them in 2 hours 0 minutes 45.629 seconds.[^c45] At that moment Frentzen was sitting in the pits with 74 laps to his name, one fewer than the top three.[^c21] Motor Sport's 1996 report argued that Sauber might even have won had they fitted Frentzen with slicks when he asked for them.[^c42]

So only three cars crossed the line under the flag: Panis, David Coulthard and Johnny Herbert.[^c22] The classification is less clear-cut. The results database lists seven: Frentzen fourth, Salo fifth, Häkkinen sixth, Irvine seventh. Motor Sport's 2026 retrospective, by contrast, speaks of just three classified finishers.[^c23]

## The finish

Coulthard finished just 4.828 seconds behind Panis and kept up the pressure to the last lap.[^c24]

> "There was a lot of pressure at the end from David."[^c25]

Coulthard drove the race in a helmet borrowed from Schumacher, because his own kept misting up.[^c26] Afterwards he was under no illusions:

> "It was just a case of getting the car home and getting points."[^c43]

Herbert came home third without overtaking anyone all race.[^c27]

Panis's fuel gamble hung by a thread. Near the end his engineer warned him the tank was almost empty, and after the finish a mechanic found nothing left in it.[^c28] Panis refused an extra stop. He asked his engineer for a per-lap fuel target, then short-shifted, did without sixth gear and coasted through the harbour chicane.[^c40] The ten points for the win went to a Frenchman who had started from the seventh row.[^c53] By the flag fuel was the last thing on his mind:

> "As I crossed the line I gave a cry of joy into the intercom."[^c29]

> "I'm delighted to win for Ligier."[^c29]

## Ligier's last win

Ligier had raced in Formula 1 since 1976 and had eight wins before Monaco, six of them scored by Jacques Laffite.[^c50] This was the first since 1981, when the same Laffite won the Canadian Grand Prix.[^c30] GrandPrix.com added another detail: no French driver in a French car had won at Monaco since 1930, when René Dreyfus took the flag first in a Bugatti.[^c31] For Panis himself, Monaco remained the only win of his Formula 1 career.[^c32]

[[media:m3]]

It came at the team's hardest moment. Flavio Briatore had bought Ligier in early 1994, and it was under him that Panis got a proper two-year contract for 1995 and 1996.[^c44] But money was still short. In the Monaco paddock there were rumours the team could close unless an investor turned up quickly, and Briatore still owned 85% of the company.[^c33] Around the same time, in May 1996, Alain Prost, then linked with McLaren, said he would like to have his own team one day, adding:

> "To make a competitive team is very difficult."[^c51]

Less than a year later, in February 1997, Prost bought the team from Briatore, and it became Prost Grand Prix.[^c34] The new owner immediately secured Peugeot engines from 1998.[^c46] And so Monaco 1996 became the ninth and last win in Ligier's history, and 1996 the last season under that name.[^c35]
