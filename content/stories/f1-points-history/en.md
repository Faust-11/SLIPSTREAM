## 1950: eight for a win, but not every race counts

In the first world championship in 1950 only the top five scored: 8 for a win, then 6, 4, 3 and 2.[^c1] One more point went to the fastest lap, regardless of where the driver finished.[^c2]

Not every result counted. From 1950 to 1953 only a driver's four best results were included.[^c3] Luigi Fagioli scored 28 points over the seven rounds of 1950, but was credited with 24 in the table and finished third.[^c4] The "best results" rule survived in various forms until 1990.[^c5]

The fastest-lap bonus was let down by the timing. At the 1954 British Grand Prix the same best time, 1:50, was credited to seven drivers, and each got one-seventh of a point. They included Juan Manuel Fangio, Alberto Ascari, Stirling Moss and Mike Hawthorn.[^c6] Until 1958 drivers who shared a car also shared its points.[^c7]

## 1960–1990: nine for a win and dropped scores

In 1960 a point was added for sixth place, and the fastest-lap bonus was dropped.[^c8] In 1961 a win went up to nine points.[^c9] In the constructors' standings that year, though, a win was still worth eight.[^c10] The nine-point scale lasted until 1990, longer than any other scale in F1 history.[^c11]

How many results counted changed almost every year. In 1979 the best eight counted: four from the first seven races and four from the last eight.[^c12] From 1981 to 1990 the best 11 results counted, even though a season could have 16 races.[^c13]

Because of this, twice the champion was not the driver who had scored more points. In 1964 Graham Hill scored one point more than John Surtees, but had to drop his fifth place in Belgium and lost the title 39–40.[^c14] In 1988 Alain Prost scored 105 points and Ayrton Senna 94.[^c15] But only the best 11 results counted, so Senna became champion on 90 points to Prost's 87.[^c16] By RaceFans' count, Prost dropped 18 points and Senna only four.[^c17]

## 1991: ten for a win, everything counts

In 1991 the best-results rule was scrapped and a win became worth ten points. That scale was used until 2002.[^c18] Senna won the first four ten-point races in a row.[^c19]

In 2002 Michael Schumacher sealed the title as early as the French Grand Prix on 21 July, with six rounds of the season to spare.[^c20] Rubens Barrichello, second in the championship, finished 67 points behind him.[^c21]

## 2003: points down to eighth

From 2003 the top eight scored: 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1.[^c22] Motor Sport and RaceFans link the change to Schumacher's dominance: second place was now only two points behind first.[^c23] In 2003 Schumacher won his sixth title by two points from Kimi Räikkönen.[^c24]

## 2010: 25 for a win

In December 2009 the FIA announced a new scale for the top ten: 25-20-15-10-8-6-5-3-2-1.[^c25] A month later the Sporting Working Group backed widening the gap between first and second to seven points.[^c26] Red Bull team principal Christian Horner said at the time: "I think you should never be afraid of change so long as it is a change for the better."[^c27] In early February 2010 the 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 scale was agreed, which the FIA said was meant to encourage "the race to win". On 12 February the FIA published the season's regulations with it.[^c28]

## Abu Dhabi 2014: double points

In December 2013 it was announced that points for the final race of the 2014 season would be doubled.[^c29] According to AP, the idea was Bernie Ecclestone's: he wanted viewers to keep watching the championship until the last race.[^c30] Sky Sports writes that Ecclestone had originally wanted double points at each of the last three races.[^c31]

Lewis Hamilton won the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix and received 50 points for it.[^c32] AP calculated that Hamilton could have lost the title because of double points had Nico Rosberg won and Hamilton finished third.[^c33] But doubling did not change the champion: Hamilton would have won the title without it.[^c34] In early December 2014 the FIA World Motor Sport Council scrapped double points.[^c35]

## 2019–2024: a point for fastest lap

On 11 March 2019 the FIA announced that a point for fastest lap was returning. Only a driver classified in the top ten could get it, and another point went to his team. The FIA World Motor Sport Council approved the change on 7 March subject to an e-vote by the Strategy Group and the F1 Commission, which gave unanimous approval. It added 21 points to a season.[^c36] The bonus had last been awarded in 1959.[^c37] The first to receive it was Valtteri Bottas: for his win and fastest lap in Australia he scored 26 points.[^c38]

The bonus was most often taken with a late pit stop: a driver with a big cushion over the car behind came into the pits for fresh tyres and set the best time.[^c39] At the 2024 Singapore Grand Prix Daniel Ricciardo of RB did just that. He finished outside the top ten and scored nothing himself, but took the bonus away from Lando Norris and so helped Red Bull's Max Verstappen.[^c40] In October 2024 the FIA announced that from 2025 there would be no fastest-lap bonus.[^c41]

## Sprints

Sprints arrived in 2021 at three events, and only the top three scored there: 3, 2 and 1.[^c42] In 2022 points were extended to the top eight: from 8 for a win down to one for eighth place.[^c43]

## Half points without a single lap

For shortened races a simple rule applied for a long time: points were awarded once two laps had been completed, and if less than 75% of the distance had been covered, half points.[^c44] At the 2021 Belgian Grand Prix, because of heavy rain, the cars completed a few laps behind the safety car only.[^c45] Max Verstappen got 12.5 points for the win and George Russell 9 for second, although there was not a single racing lap.[^c46]

After the criticism the rule was rewritten. From 2022 only laps under racing conditions count, and the points depend on how much of the distance has been covered. Up to 25%, the top five score on a 6-4-3-2-1 scale. Between 25% and 50%, the winner gets 13 points and the top nine score. Between 50% and 75%, the winner gets 19 points and the top ten score.[^c47]

At its first big test the new system misfired. The race at Suzuka in 2022 was red-flagged, then restarted, and a little more than half the distance was completed by the finish.[^c48] Teams expected the winner to get 19 points, but full points were awarded.[^c49] "The rules regarding the reduced points allocation (article 6.5) only apply in the event of race suspension that cannot be resumed," an FIA spokesperson explained.[^c50] Verstappen got 25 points and became world champion for the second time with four races of the season still to go.[^c51] In February 2023 the F1 Commission changed the wording to "ensure that shorter races have reduced points even if they don't finish with a suspended race".[^c52]

## What next

In spring 2024 Autosport reported a proposal to award points to the top 12 drivers.[^c53] Martin Brundle was against it: "Points must be hard won and treasured. Valued. Not some kind of lucky dip where everyone wins a prize."[^c54] The scale has not changed: at the 2026 Brazilian Grand Prix the winner received 25 points, only the top ten scored, and there was no longer a fastest-lap bonus.[^c55]
