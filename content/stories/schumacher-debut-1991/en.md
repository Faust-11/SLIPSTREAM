## A canister at Hyde Park Corner

Michael Schumacher's Formula 1 debut began with a road-rage row in London. In December 1990 Jordan driver Bertrand Gachot clipped a taxi at Hyde Park Corner, and in the argument that followed he sprayed CS gas into the cab driver's face.[^c2]

The court found him guilty of causing actual bodily harm. According to Grandprix.com's 1991 account, Gachot got 18 months for the assault and six for possessing the gas; RacingNews365 reports 18 months, PlanetF1 two years.[^c3] He never accepted the verdict:

> "For me it was a clear case of self-defence."[^c4]

About the sentence itself he was even briefer: "It was an incredible sentence."[^c4]

Gachot was jailed in August 1991, just before the Belgian Grand Prix.[^c5] While the teams headed for the Ardennes, he was in Brixton Prison in London.[^c6]

## A rookie who brought money

Jordan had a seat to fill. Eddie Jordan wanted Stefan Johansson, but the Swede wanted to be paid.[^c7] Instead the drive went to 22-year-old Michael Schumacher, a Mercedes sportscar driver.[^c8] A letter of intent dated 22 August 1991 states that PP Sauber Ltd would pay Jordan £150,000 per race for that season.[^c9]

There was one problem: Schumacher had never raced at Spa. His manager Willi Weber later admitted:

> "Eddie wanted to know if he had ever driven at Spa, so I told him 'I think about 100 times', which was a complete lie."[^c10]

Before the race Jordan squeezed in a short test on Silverstone's South Circuit. Schumacher did about 20 laps, and according to technical chief Gary Anderson his fastest lap was as good as the team had ever done at that track.[^c11] Andrea de Cesaris was supposed to take him round the circuit in a road car, but instead simply explained which gears to use in which corners.[^c45] So Schumacher learned Spa on a fold-out bicycle.[^c12] The rookie and his manager stayed in a youth hostel. "There was no hotel booking for us, so we went to a youth hostel," Weber recalled.[^c13]

## Seventh on the grid

The 1991 Belgian Grand Prix, round 11 of the season, fell on 25 August.[^c1] In the first qualifying session, on Friday, Schumacher was eighth.[^c14] He started seventh, behind both McLarens of Ayrton Senna and Gerhard Berger, both Ferraris of Alain Prost and Jean Alesi, Nigel Mansell's Williams and Nelson Piquet's Benetton.[^c15] His team-mate, the veteran de Cesaris, was 11th, four places and seven-tenths of a second behind.[^c16] The Italian later recalled:

> "Straight away he was as quick as me."[^c17]

According to de Cesaris, Anderson came over and told him that Michael was taking Blanchimont flat and he wasn't.[^c46]

In Sunday morning's warm-up Schumacher was fourth fastest.[^c18] In its October issue, Motor Sport wrote that he "drove with an elan that wholly belied his complete lack of experience in the category".[^c35]

## A few hundred metres

Schumacher's race was over almost before it began: he did not complete a single lap. Nearly every source blames the clutch; Schumacher's official website speaks of the gearshift.[^c19] Gary Anderson later took his share of the blame: "it was also our problem because we had a very fragile clutch that year."[^c20] Schumacher himself had no doubts:

> "If I had actually done the whole race and finished the race, I certainly would have been on the podium."[^c21]

In Formula1.com's view he had a real chance: de Cesaris was running second and catching Senna when his engine failed three laps from the end.[^c22] Senna won, Berger was second, Piquet third, and Benetton's other driver Roberto Moreno fourth.[^c23] Moreno also set the fastest lap at Spa.[^c24] Within a week Benetton had decided to drop him immediately.[^c44]

## One word

On the Monday before the Italian Grand Prix, Jordan was expecting Schumacher's representatives to sign a full contract. The next morning Eddie Jordan received a fax: "I'm very sorry but I'm not going to be able to drive for your team."[^c25]

The key lay in that letter of intent. In the original, Schumacher promised to sign an agreement with Jordan before Monza covering 1991, 1992 and 1993.[^c47] The words "the driver agreement" had been changed to "a driver agreement".[^c26] Eddie Jordan said years later:

> "It is incredible that big things in F1 can come down to the difference between using the word 'a' in a contract when it should have been 'the'."[^c27]

On the Wednesday Schumacher tested the Benetton at Silverstone, and the contract was signed.[^c28] At Monza, Benetton's Tom Walkinshaw summed up the team's position: "He was a free agent."[^c29]

## Courts before Monza

Jordan went to the High Court in London to stop Schumacher driving for Benetton, and lost.[^c30] Moreno, whom Benetton had dropped for the rookie, did obtain an injunction from an Italian court.[^c31]

Bernie Ecclestone cut the knot: he gathered all sides at a hotel in Italy, and Moreno accepted a pay-off of around half a million dollars.[^c32] Eddie Jordan, according to The Race, advised him to hold out for a million.[^c48] Flavio Briatore later admitted Moreno's contract had been badly drafted: "It was not a good contract. I put a lot of mistakes."[^c49]

Nelson Piquet rebelled too: according to Briatore, he refused to drive at Monza. Briatore had a seat fitted for Alessandro Zanardi, and Piquet backed down.[^c50] On 8 September at Monza, Schumacher started for Benetton and Moreno for Jordan.[^c33] Schumacher finished fifth, one place ahead of Piquet, and scored his first points.[^c34] He never drove for Jordan again.[^c43]

Gachot was released after two months and four missed grands prix. The Court of Appeal did not quash the conviction, but considered the sentence much too harsh.[^c36] That same day Gachot flew to Japan, for the Grand Prix at Suzuka.[^c51] By then he no longer had a Formula 1 seat.[^c37]

## A living room in the Ardennes

Schumacher came from Kerpen, not far over the Belgian border.[^c42] He took his first Formula 1 win at Spa, on 30 August 1992.[^c38] In all he won there six times, more than anyone else in the circuit's history.[^c39] Almost exactly 13 years after his debut, at the same track, he sealed a record seventh world title.[^c40]

Spa is often called Schumacher's "living room". His official website adds a nuance: at first the circuit was his "youth room", and it became his "living room" after that first win.[^c41]
