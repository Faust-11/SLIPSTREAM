On TV, a Formula 1 team is two drivers, a boss on the pit wall and twenty mechanics in the garage. In reality each car is backed by hundreds of people, and at the big teams by well over a thousand. Here is how many there are, what they do and what the budget cap did to them.

## From a few hundred to over a thousand

Motorsport.com gives the general frame: an F1 team directly involves between 300 and 1,200 people.[^c1] More precise numbers can be found at Companies House, the UK company register. Most teams are based in England and file annual accounts there that include their average headcount.

At Mercedes-Benz Grand Prix Ltd, the company behind the Mercedes team, an average of 1,348 people were employed in 2025: 998 in design, manufacturing and engineering and 350 in administration. A year earlier the figure was 1,323.[^c2] Mercedes engines are built by a separate company, Mercedes AMG High Performance Powertrains. In 2025 it employed another 886 people on average, 796 of them in production. The same company supplies power units to McLaren, Aston Martin and Williams and carries out R&D for road cars.[^c3]

The Red Bull Technology group, whose accounts consolidate Red Bull Racing Ltd, averaged 1,100 employees in 2025: 456 in racing and production, 303 in design and 341 in administration. In 2024 there were 969.[^c4] Williams Grand Prix Engineering reported 1,151 people in 2025 (765 in research and development, 300 in administration and support, 86 in marketing), against 949 a year earlier.[^c5]

At the opposite end sits Haas. In December 2025 PlanetF1 reported that the team's staff count had grown from 230 to around 380 in two years. In November 2026 Autosport put it at "around 400", still by far the smallest operation on the grid.[^c6]

These numbers are not fully comparable: each group splits its people between companies in its own way, and the categories in the accounts differ. But the order of magnitude is clear: a big team is more than a thousand people, the smallest a few hundred.

## Who works at the factory

Most of these people never go to a race. Motorsport.com describes a typical factory like this: a design office with aerodynamics split into three or four sub-teams; a simulation department that covers more than 100,000 virtual kilometres a year; manufacturing; test and development; and a race bay where cars are stripped, cleaned and rebuilt between races.[^c7] The factory keeps working on race weekends too: at Mercedes, strategists sit in the Race Support Room in Brackley.[^c8]

## Who goes to the track

By Motorsport.com's estimate, around 75 people travel to each race.[^c9] Those who work directly on the cars are limited by the Sporting Regulations. In 2025 a team could have no more than 58 "operational" personnel at the circuit; for 2026 the limit was raised to 60. According to the FIA, the change is meant to let teams operate the new generation of cars effectively.[^c10]

The limit applies from 29 hours before the first practice session until two hours after the start of the race. Only in the window from 45 minutes before the first formation lap until 15 minutes after the start is the number of people unlimited.[^c11] Drivers, as well as hospitality, marketing, media, security staff and truck drivers, do not count towards the limit.[^c12] Separately, each team gets 16 exceptions per season for trainees, but no trainee may attend more than three events in that capacity.[^c13]

In the pandemic year of 2020 the rules were stricter: at events without spectators a team was allowed 80 people, 60 of them operational, and from the Belgian Grand Prix the limit was raised to 90.[^c14]

Among those who travel are the engineers. Mercedes has around 15 to 20 engineers at the track. The race engineer is the link between the team and the driver, the performance engineer works alongside him analysing telemetry, and separate engineers look after the electronics and the power unit.[^c15]

## The pit stop: twenty-odd pairs of hands

More than 20 people take part in a pit stop. One lifts the car on the front jack, another on the rear jack. Four operate wheel guns, four remove the old wheels and four more fit the new ones. Two hold the car steady, two adjust the front wing, and one controls the release.[^c16] In 2019 F1.com described a Williams crew of 23 people including reserves.[^c17]

This is not a separate profession. Almost everyone in the pit crew is a mechanic with another job in the team.[^c18] They train a lot: before the 2019 season Williams practised more than 1,200 pit stops on a rig.[^c19] The fastest pit stop in history took 1.80 seconds: McLaren serviced Lando Norris's car at the Qatar Grand Prix on 8 October 2023.[^c20]

## What the budget cap did

The spending cap in force since 2021 hit the workforce of the big teams first. At Mercedes, average headcount fell from 1,063 in 2020 to 1,004 in 2021. Design, manufacturing and engineering lost people, going from 906 to 831, while administration actually grew, from 157 to 173.[^c21]

Sources disagree on how many people left Mercedes: GP Racing reported "just under 50" in 2021, while Crash.net later gave 59.[^c22] At Red Bull, according to Motorsport-Total.com figures relayed by Crash.net, 154 people were made redundant at Red Bull Racing and Red Bull Technologies in 2020–2021, though not all of them worked for the F1 team.[^c23]

Red Bull boss Christian Horner said in April 2021 that the team's staff numbers were now "in the 800s" and that it had gone through "the pain of redundancies" over the winter. Toto Wolff admitted it had been no easier at Mercedes:

> "You cannot secure your capability in terms of people if you suddenly have to operate under a drastic cost cap."[^c24]

Some people were moved rather than let go. The Financial Regulations do not count spending on non-F1 activities, or on the HR, finance and legal functions, towards the cap.[^c25] In 2021, 30 Mercedes staff worked on an America's Cup yacht, while Ferrari moved Scuderia specialists onto road-car development.[^c26]

Within a few years the numbers were climbing again. In 2025 Mercedes employed 1,348 people, more than before the cap, 350 of them in administration, twice as many as in 2021.[^c2][^c21] The accounts do not say whether this is linked to the exemptions in the rules.
