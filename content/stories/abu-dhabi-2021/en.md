The 2021 season arrived in Abu Dhabi as a dead heat. Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton were level on 369.5 points, so the title would go to whoever finished ahead.[^c1] The race at Yas Marina on 12 December ran over 58 laps, with Verstappen on pole and Hamilton alongside.[^c2] What happened in the closing minutes of that race kept the FIA busy for months and cost the race director his job.

## A race Hamilton was already winning

Hamilton took the lead at the start and controlled the race from there. With six laps to go he was around 12 seconds clear.[^c3] Horner openly admitted his team needed a miracle.[^c37] It arrived in the form of a Williams.

On lap 53 Nicholas Latifi hit the barrier at the exit of Turn 14, and the safety car came out.[^c4] Red Bull immediately called Verstappen in for fresh soft tyres. Mercedes would not risk track position and left Hamilton out on worn hards.[^c5] The recovery dragged on, partly because the Williams' brakes had caught fire.[^c6] The laps ticked away, and the race looked set to finish behind the safety car, which meant a Hamilton win.

## Five cars and one lap

Five lapped cars sat between the leaders. On lap 56 race control sent the teams the standard message: lapped cars would not be allowed to overtake.[^c7] At Red Bull it sounded like a verdict. Christian Horner got on the radio to race director Michael Masi:

> "Why aren't we getting these lapped cars out of the way? … You only need one racing lap."[^c8]

On lap 57 the call changed. The lapped cars were allowed to pass the safety car, but only the five running between Hamilton and Verstappen.[^c9] They were Lando Norris, Fernando Alonso, Esteban Ocon, Charles Leclerc and Sebastian Vettel. Three more lapped cars, those of Daniel Ricciardo, Lance Stroll and Mick Schumacher, stayed behind Verstappen, out of eight lapped cars in all.[^c10] Toto Wolff came on the radio to race control:

> "Michael, this isn't right."[^c11]

The safety car pitted at the end of that same lap 57, and the drivers began the final lap with nothing between them.[^c12] Verstappen, on fresh softs, attacked at once and got past Hamilton on the brakes into Turn 5. He took the chequered flag first.[^c13] Before he had even reached the finish, Hamilton told his engineer:

> "This has been manipulated."[^c14]

Wolff demanded that Masi go back to the order of the previous lap. The race director's reply was short:

> "Toto, it's called a motor race."[^c15]

Hamilton still congratulated his rival in parc fermé.[^c38] In the final standings Verstappen had 395.5 points, Hamilton 387.5.[^c16] The Netherlands had its first world champion,[^c17] and Hamilton was denied a record eighth title.[^c18] The constructors' trophy that season went to Mercedes.[^c19]

## Protests: two "no"s in one evening

Mercedes lodged two protests. The first concerned overtaking under the safety car (Article 48.8 of the sporting regulations), the second the lapped-car procedure itself (Article 48.12).[^c20]

The stewards dealt with the first quickly. They accepted that Verstappen had, for a very short moment, been slightly ahead of Hamilton while both were accelerating and braking, but found that he dropped back and was behind at the line where the safety car period ended. The protest was dismissed.[^c21]

The second took longer. Horner, team manager Jonathan Wheatley and Adrian Newey attended for Red Bull. At 20:50 the hearing was adjourned so Red Bull could prepare its response, and it resumed at 21:30 with the race director present.[^c39] Red Bull argued that "any" lapped cars in the rule did not mean "all", that Article 48.13 on the "Safety Car in this lap" signal overrode 48.12, and that the race director had the final word on the safety car.[^c22] Masi explained his reasoning: the teams had long agreed it was desirable to finish races under green flags rather than behind the safety car.[^c23]

At 23:03 local time the stewards dismissed this protest too. They accepted that Article 48.12 may not have been applied in full but ruled that 48.13 overrode it. Mercedes' request to rewrite the classification as it stood after the penultimate lap was described as effectively shortening the race retrospectively.[^c24]

At 23:22 Mercedes gave notice that it would appeal to the FIA International Court of Appeal. The deadline to lodge the appeal was 16 December 2021.[^c25] On that day the team dropped the appeal, saying instead: "We will hold the FIA accountable for this process."[^c26]

## Masi out, a "VAR" in

On 17 February 2022 the new FIA president, Mohammed Ben Sulayem, announced changes. Masi would be offered another position within the federation, while Niels Wittich and Eduardo Freitas would alternate as race director, with Herbie Blash as permanent senior adviser.[^c27] The FIA also announced a virtual race control room modelled on football's VAR and took the teams' live radio exchanges with the race director off the TV broadcast.[^c28] Masi had run the races since March 2019, when Charlie Whiting died.[^c29]

## "Human error"

The FIA published its final analysis on 19 March 2022. The key finding: lapped cars had until then been identified manually, and human error meant not all of them were allowed to unlap themselves.[^c30] At the same time the FIA stressed that the race director had acted in good faith.[^c31] The central breach was stated plainly: Masi called the safety car in without it completing an additional lap, as the regulations required.[^c32]

Nobody intended to revisit the result. The FIA said the race and championship results were "valid, final and cannot now be changed".[^c33] The report also noted that the radio messages from Horner and Wolff in the final laps distracted the race director when he had to make difficult decisions under time pressure.[^c34] For 2022 the FIA introduced software that automatically produces the list of lapped cars, and the word "any" in the rule was replaced by "all".[^c35]

## What remains

Abu Dhabi 2021 overturned no result and changed no line in the standings. It did change how Formula 1 is refereed. The FIA's own report acknowledged that too many roles had piled up on the race director over the years, and that Articles 48.12 and 48.13 could be read in different ways.[^c36] So the job was split between several people, radio contact with race control was restricted, and the rule was rewritten. Whether that title was "fair" is something fans will argue about for a long time. The FIA documents answer a narrower question: the race director made a mistake, and the result stands.
