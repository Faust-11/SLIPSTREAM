## The first night race

The Singapore Grand Prix on 28 September 2008 was Formula 1's first night race.[^c8] Going in, Hamilton had 78 points and Massa 77, while Alonso's Renault was seventh on 28.[^c9]

Alonso was quick in practice, but early in the second part of qualifying his fuel system failed and he started 15th.[^c10] Renault fuelled him very light, for only 12 to 15 laps.[^c11]

## The race

Alonso pitted as early as lap 12 and rejoined last.[^c12] Two laps later his teammate Nelson Piquet Jr. hit the wall on the exit of turn 17.[^c13] The FIA's official decision says lap 14; some reports say lap 13.[^c14]

[[media:m2]]

In 2008 the pit lane closed when the safety car came out, until the field had lined up behind it.[^c15] Rivals could only refuel later, while Alonso, already fuelled, rose to fifth.[^c16] Rosberg and Kubica, running out of fuel, pitted while the lane was closed and were penalised.[^c17] Sources differ on the type of penalty.[^c18]

Leader Massa left his stop with the fuel hose still attached and stopped at the end of the pit lane while mechanics ran to pull it off.[^c19] Sources differ on how many mechanics he knocked over.[^c20]

Jarno Trulli led until his stop on lap 33, and from lap 34 Alonso was in front.[^c21] He won, Rosberg was second and Hamilton third. Massa finished 13th with no points.[^c22]

## How it came out

As early as 2008 the driver's father, Nelson Piquet, privately told an FIA official the crash might have been staged. The FIA decided there was not enough evidence.[^c23] On 26 July 2009, the day of the Hungarian Grand Prix, he went to the FIA directly.[^c24] On 30 July Piquet Jr. gave a sworn statement in Paris.[^c25] Only after that did it emerge that he had been dropped, and on 18 August Renault named his replacement.[^c26]

> «Mr. Symonds, in the presence of Mr. Briatore, asked me if I would be willing to sacrifice my race for the team by "causing a safety car".»[^c27]
> — Nelson Piquet Jr. · statement to the FIA, 30.07.2009

The spot was chosen because cranes would take long to reach it, making a safety car certain.[^c28] The telemetry showed Piquet applied the throttle earlier and harder, and when the wheels spun he went back to full throttle and held it.[^c29]

Accounts differ on whose idea it was. According to Piquet, on race day he was called to Briatore's office, where Symonds, with Briatore present, asked him to «cause a safety car».[^c30] Symonds maintained that Piquet himself raised the idea the day before, on Saturday; he did not answer the stewards' key questions.[^c31] Witness X, a Renault employee whose name was withheld, heard Symonds discuss Briatore's plan on Saturday and objected. His evidence was key to the finding against Briatore.[^c32]

[[media:m3]]

In late August the Belgian GP stewards interviewed Renault staff.[^c33] On 11 September Renault and Briatore filed a criminal complaint in France against both Piquets, but by 16 September the team had told the FIA it would not contest the charges, and Briatore and Symonds had left.[^c34]

## The ruling

On 21 September 2009 the FIA World Council gave Renault a disqualification suspended until the end of 2011. Briatore was banned indefinitely and Symonds for five years; Piquet had immunity and Alonso was found not involved.[^c35] The team was not fined but paid for the investigation and made a significant contribution to FIA safety projects.[^c36] The FIA also refused licences to any team employing Briatore and superlicence renewals to drivers he managed.[^c37]

> «They not only compromised the integrity of the sport but also endangered the lives of spectators, officials, other competitors and Nelson Piquet Jr. himself.»[^c38]
> — FIA World Council decision · 21.09.2009

> «I bitterly regret my actions to follow the orders I was given.»[^c39]
> — Nelson Piquet Jr. · statement, 21.09.2009

On 24 September ING and Mutua Madrileña ended their contracts with the team.[^c40] On 5 January 2010 a Paris court ruled the sanctions on Briatore and Symonds unlawful: they had not been allowed a proper defence, the decision relied on anonymous evidence, and neither held an FIA licence. The court did not examine the conspiracy itself.[^c41][^c42] The damages were token; sources differ on the amounts.[^c43] On 12 April 2010 the parties settled: both accepted a share of responsibility and agreed not to hold operational roles in Formula 1 until the end of 2012.[^c44]

## A title by one point

Hamilton won the 2008 title on 98 points to Massa's 97.[^c54] Had the Singapore result been cancelled, Hamilton would have lost his 6 points for third and Massa nothing: 92 to 97.[^c55]

In March 2023 Bernie Ecclestone told F1-Insider that he and Max Mosley knew about the crash during the 2008 season and chose to do nothing to protect the sport.[^c56] He later said he did not remember giving that interview.[^c57]

In 2024 Massa sued the FIA, Formula One Management and Ecclestone in the High Court in London for about £64m.[^c58] Sources give different filing dates.[^c59] In November 2025 a judge refused to revisit the championship result but let part of the claim go on.[^c60] By March 2026 only the conspiracy claim remained, and the point of law went to the UK Supreme Court.[^c61] The hearing there is set for 2–3 February 2027.[^c62]

> «The present claim cannot of course rewrite the outcome of the 2008 drivers' world championship.»[^c63]
> — Mr Justice Jay, High Court, London · 20.11.2025

In May 2025 Flavio Briatore, an adviser at Alpine, temporarily took over the duties of team principal at the team that grew out of that same Renault.[^c64]
