## Saturday: the pole that wasn't

On 12 May 2012 the fastest lap in qualifying for the Spanish Grand Prix was set by Lewis Hamilton: 1:21.707.[^c2] Second, more than half a second behind, was Pastor Maldonado in the Williams.[^c2] For a team that had scored just five points the season before and finished ninth in the constructors' standings, even the front row was an event.[^c8] Maldonado later recalled that the team only received a big upgrade package (floor, sidepods, front wing) on Saturday morning, and the car worked straight away.[^c40]

By the evening the timesheet had been rewritten. After qualifying Hamilton stopped his McLaren out on the circuit, apparently short of fuel.[^c3] A driver has to supply a fuel sample after the session, and the car could no longer do that.[^c3] The stewards ruled a breach of Article 6.6.2 of the FIA Technical Regulations, deleted all of Hamilton's qualifying times and allowed him to start only from the back of the grid.[^c3] Blaming a technical problem did not help:

> "As the amount of fuel put into the car is under the complete control of the competitor, the stewards cannot accept this as a case of force majeure."[^c4]

McLaren team principal Martin Whitmarsh explained that the car had been stopped with 1.3 litres left in the tank.[^c5] Pole passed to Maldonado, and it turned out to be the first and only one of his career.[^c6] The previous time a Williams had started from the front was in November 2010, when Nico Hülkenberg took pole in Brazil.[^c7]

That same weekend the team threw a surprise party for its founder, Sir Frank Williams, to mark his 70th birthday.[^c9] The coincidence of the anniversary and the win later fuelled conspiracy theories that the result had been "gifted", but nothing ever backed them up.[^c39]

Before the race Maldonado kept his own pep talk simple: "I have always been fighting for victories. I have won so many races at the time, and this is only one more. So do your job, and do what you enjoy."[^c42] To reporters he put it more briefly: "For sure I will try to win."[^c43]

## Sunday: 66 laps with Alonso in the mirrors

Maldonado's race on 13 May at the Circuit de Catalunya began badly: Fernando Alonso, alongside him on the front row, made the better start and led into the first corner.[^c1][^c10] Ferrari led the race, but Williams answered with strategy.[^c16] In the second round of pit stops Maldonado came in earlier and got ahead of Alonso with the undercut.[^c11] According to RaceFans, the Williams driver pitted on lap 25 and Alonso on lap 27, after which Maldonado was in the lead.[^c12] Alonso got stuck in traffic after his stop, and within two laps the Williams was about seven seconds clear.[^c44]

The final pit stop nearly ruined everything: a rear wheel would not go on properly and the stop dragged on. RaceFans says it was the right rear, Motor Sport says the left rear.[^c13] Alonso cut the gap to six tenths, but Maldonado kept pulling away in the final sector and stayed out of DRS range.[^c14]

At the flag the margin was 3.195 seconds. Kimi Räikkönen was third for Lotus, 3.884 seconds behind the winner.[^c15] Maldonado led 37 of the 66 laps, Alonso 27 and Räikkönen two.[^c16] The Finn was on a three-stop strategy, chased the leaders down on fresh tyres and took 1.6 seconds out of Alonso on the last lap, but fell less than a second short of second place.[^c45] Hamilton climbed from 24th on the grid to eighth, despite running over a wheel gun as he left his pit box on lap 15.[^c21][^c46] After five rounds Alonso and Sebastian Vettel shared the championship lead on 61 points each.[^c47]

## Eight years without a win

Williams' previous win had come at the 2004 Brazilian Grand Prix, when Juan Pablo Montoya won.[^c17] In the results database this is the team's 114th victory, and Williams has not won a Grand Prix since.[^c18] Maldonado became the first Venezuelan to win a Formula 1 race.[^c19] The win also remained the only podium of his career.[^c37] Venezuela's then president, Hugo Chávez, phoned to congratulate him.[^c41]

> "It's a wonderful day for the team which has been pushing so hard."[^c20]

"It was my first podium and my first victory. You can imagine what I feel," he added.[^c48] For Venezuela, he said, it was "a great moment for my country after nearly 30 years without any driver in Formula One."[^c49] He later explained that baseball is the main sport at home, so a Formula 1 win was something entirely new for the country.[^c50]

Fourteen years later, in an interview for Williams, Maldonado spoke most about Frank Williams: "And to give him his last victory in Formula 1 – for me, is something very, very special."[^c36]

## Smoke over the pit lane

The celebrations did not last. While Frank Williams was addressing the team, a fire broke out in the Williams garage.[^c22] Sources differ on how long after the finish: The National says about an hour, RacingNews365 about 90 minutes, PlanetF1 "less than two hours".[^c23] The fire started at the refuelling equipment. AP and Motor Sport reported a fuel leak, RacingNews365 a fuel rig failure.[^c24] The team's reserve driver Valtteri Bottas described it:

> "I felt an explosion from behind, somewhere from the fuel area, and everyone ran out quickly."[^c25]

Mechanics from neighbouring teams rushed to fight the fire alongside the circuit's firefighters.[^c28] Force India deputy team principal Bob Fernley criticised the circuit's slow response: "My guy got there before the official fire fighters."[^c32] Frank Williams, who used a wheelchair, was taken out of the garage immediately.[^c38]

According to the FIA, the circuit medical staff saw 31 people, and seven were sent to hospital.[^c26] Williams reported four of its own staff hurt, three of whom remained in hospital. Caterham had four staff members examined.[^c27] The fire damaged Bruno Senna's car.[^c29] Maldonado's winning car survived: it was not in the garage but parked with the other podium finishers' cars.[^c52] The FIA said it was working with the Spanish authorities investigating the incident.[^c51]

Maldonado himself carried his cousin Manuel out of the smoke-filled garage on his back that evening.[^c30] According to PlanetF1, the boy was 12 and had a broken foot.[^c31]

## A season in which everyone won

Maldonado's win fitted into an unusual spring. After five rounds of 2012, the five races had been won by five different drivers from five different teams: Jenson Button (McLaren), Alonso (Ferrari), Nico Rosberg (Mercedes), Vettel (Red Bull) and Maldonado (Williams).[^c35] Then Mark Webber won in Monaco and Hamilton in Canada, making it seven different winners in seven races.[^c33] AP called that level of parity unprecedented.[^c34]

[[media:m2]]

Even in that run, the Barcelona win looked the least likely: a team that had scored five points the year before beat Ferrari and Lotus on track.[^c8][^c15] That same evening its garage was on fire. And the result itself has remained Williams' last win.[^c18]
