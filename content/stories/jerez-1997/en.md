## Before Jerez

The 1997 finale moved from Estoril to Jerez after the FIA said the Portuguese circuit had not met its requirements.[^c7] Going into the last race Jacques Villeneuve (Williams) had seven wins, Michael Schumacher (Ferrari) five.[^c8]

At Suzuka, two weeks before the finale, Villeneuve failed to slow for waved yellows in Saturday practice. It was his fourth such offence of the year, and a suspended ban from Monza kicked in.[^c9] Williams appealed, Villeneuve raced and finished fifth, but on 16 October, under FIA pressure, the team withdrew the appeal. He lost those 2 points: 77 to Schumacher's 78.[^c10]

On equal points Villeneuve would rank higher on wins. So Schumacher only had to finish ahead of Villeneuve, and Villeneuve had to score and finish ahead of Schumacher.[^c11] Williams had already won the Constructors' Cup in Japan.[^c12]

## Three identical laps

In qualifying Villeneuve, Schumacher and Heinz-Harald Frentzen set exactly the same time, 1:21.072, to the thousandth.[^c13] Pole went to Villeneuve because he set it first.[^c14] Damon Hill's Arrows was fourth, 0.058 s slower.[^c15]

## The race

Schumacher took the lead at the start and Frentzen got past Villeneuve, then, on team orders, let him through early in the race.[^c16] Sources differ on whether it was lap 7 or lap 8.[^c17] Schumacher pitted on laps 22 and 43, Villeneuve on laps 23 and 44.[^c18] Between the first stops Frentzen led and held Schumacher up.[^c19]

Norberto Fontana, in a Ferrari-engined Sauber, let Schumacher by and then held Villeneuve up, by an estimated 2.5 to 3 seconds.[^c20] In 2006 Fontana said it was Jean Todt's order; Sauber and Ferrari deny it.[^c21] By the lap data, Schumacher led by 3.2 s after lap 1 and 5.2 s after lap 20, and by under half a second after lap 47.[^c22]

## Dry Sac

On lap 48 Villeneuve braked later and dived down the inside into the Dry Sac hairpin. Schumacher turned into him: the Ferrari's right front wheel hit the Williams' left sidepod. The Ferrari beached itself in the gravel and Villeneuve drove on.[^c23] The result sheet shows Schumacher with 47 laps and «collision».[^c24] Sources give the time of the impact as 15:08 or 15:12.[^c25]

> «Either Michael had his eyes closed or his hands slipped on the steering wheel.»[^c26]
> — Jacques Villeneuve · Jerez, after the race, 26.10.1997

> «Jacques cut in and didn't try to brake at all. Certainly, I closed the door.»[^c27]
> — Michael Schumacher · after the race, 26–27.10.1997

The impact tore the Williams' battery from its mountings; it hung on by its wires.[^c28] Villeneuve feared a failure and backed off: on laps 48 to 50 he ran 1:27 to 1:29 while the McLarens did 1:25.[^c29]

## The finish

On lap 67 David Coulthard let his teammate Mika Häkkinen by,[^c30] and on the last lap Villeneuve let both McLarens through. Häkkinen took his first career win; Villeneuve finished third.[^c31] With 81 points he was world champion.[^c32] Gerhard Berger finished fourth in the last race of his career.[^c61]

On 8 November The Times published Williams radio transcripts and alleged a deal with McLaren.[^c33] The recordings were circulated by Ferrari as part of Schumacher's defence.[^c34]

Did McLaren help Williams? Sources disagree. Officially, no: the FIA World Council threw out the collusion charge, finding the result «was not fixed».[^c35] Later it was partly admitted. Coulthard said in 2021 that Ron Dennis had agreed to help Williams with the title if needed.[^c36] McLaren's Jo Ramirez recalls Patrick Head coming to their garage to say Jacques would not resist.[^c37]

## The ruling

The stewards in Jerez called the collision a racing incident.[^c38] The next day the FIA summoned Schumacher to an extraordinary World Council.[^c39] At first he blamed Villeneuve,[^c40] and on 28 October at Maranello he admitted a mistake.[^c41]

> «I am human like everyone else and unfortunately I made a mistake.»[^c42]
> — Michael Schumacher · Maranello, 28.10.1997

In Germany the RTL channel ran a phone poll: of 63,081 callers, 28% said they no longer supported Schumacher.[^c43]

On 11 November the FIA World Council met in Slough near London.[^c44] Schumacher was excluded from the final 1997 standings, and Frentzen became runner-up.[^c45] The race results, Schumacher's five wins and Ferrari's Constructors' points stood.[^c46] There was no fine and no ban for 1998.[^c47] The Council found the act deliberate but not premeditated,[^c48] and required Schumacher to take part in an FIA road-safety campaign in 1998.[^c49] Sources describe its size differently.[^c50]

> «Although the actions were deliberate they were not premeditated.»[^c51]
> — Max Mosley, FIA president · Slough, 11.11.1997

Mosley called it «much more than a slap on the wrist»,[^c52] but part of the press thought it far too lenient.[^c53]

## An echo of Adelaide

British reports from Jerez recalled the 1994 finale in Adelaide. Then Schumacher led Damon Hill by one point, they collided, both retired, and the title went to Schumacher.[^c59] Schumacher himself rejected the comparison with 1994.[^c60]
