## A race half a century in the waiting

On 11 April 1993 Formula 1 came to Donington Park in England for round three of the season, the European Grand Prix.[^c1] The last grand prix held there had been in 1938, won by Tazio Nuvolari in an Auto Union.[^c2] The return was the fulfilment of Tom Wheatcroft's dream,[^c3] and the slot opened up because a plan to stage an Asian Grand Prix at Autopolis in Japan had fallen through.[^c4]

Ayrton Senna arrived without the trump card of previous years. McLaren were running customer Ford engines, while Benetton were Ford's works team and got the better specification.[^c5] It was Senna's first season at McLaren without Honda power: the Japanese manufacturer had withdrawn from Formula 1.[^c6] By the estimate of Motor Sport's Matt Bishop, the Ford V8 was giving away at least 80bhp to the Renault V10 in the Williams.[^c7] The Williams FW15C itself had active suspension, traction control and anti-lock brakes.[^c43]

On Saturday, in the dry, Williams locked out the front row: Alain Prost took pole with Damon Hill second. Behind them lined up Michael Schumacher in the Benetton, Senna, Karl Wendlinger in the Sauber and Michael Andretti in the second McLaren.[^c8] Senna was 1.649 seconds slower than Prost in qualifying.[^c9] The day before the race he had warned:

> "There is no long straight here and passing is difficult. Because of this there may be some controversy on overtaking."[^c10]

## The lap

On Sunday the track was wet. At the start Schumacher squeezed Senna towards the left-hand edge, Wendlinger slipped down the inside into third, and Senna went into the first corner, Redgate, only fifth.[^c11]

[[media:m2]]

Senna later explained his plan:

> "At the start I decided to really go for it, before Williams had the time to settle down."[^c50]

Then it all happened in one lap. Senna passed Schumacher almost at once and went round the outside of Wendlinger through the fast Craner Curves on the run down to Old Hairpin.[^c12] The sources differ on where exactly he got past Hill: Motor Sport's 1993 report, Bishop and Motorsport.com say McLeans, while GrandPrix.com and Motor Sport in 1997 say Coppice.[^c13] He caught Prost on the straight and dived down the inside under braking for the Melbourne hairpin.[^c14] By Bishop's count, going from fifth to first took about 75 seconds.[^c15]

Further back, Andretti ran into Wendlinger and both were out on the first lap.[^c16] Rubens Barrichello's Jordan completed the opening lap fourth, from 12th on the grid.[^c17] McLaren engineer Neil Oatley recalled:

> "I remember being staggered at the size of Ayrton's lead. I thought the Williams had spun."[^c18]

Soon Senna had pulled out a lead of about seven seconds.[^c19] Prost later explained that from the start his gearbox kept dropping into neutral.[^c20]

## Rain, dry, rain again

The remaining 75 laps turned into a chess match with the weather. The track dried, and after lap 18 Senna went onto slicks. Then the rain returned, Prost and Hill went back onto wets, and Senna stayed out on slicks.[^c21] On lap 34[^c52] his pit stop went wrong: the right-rear wheel nut cross-threaded, and the lead went back to Prost.[^c22] Senna took it back on lap 39.[^c24] Prost, meanwhile, stalled at one of his stops.[^c23] Schumacher did not make half-distance: after 22 laps he slid off[^c46] when his rear wheels locked on a downshift.[^c51]

Not everyone played the tyre game so hard. Johnny Herbert's Lotus started 11th, switched to slicks early and stayed out as long as he dared.[^c47] Team director Peter Collins summed it up afterwards:

> "We kept our nerve when many others were panicked into making too many tyre stops."[^c48]

The strangest moment came on lap 57. Senna came into the pit lane for wets, but the team were not ready for him, so he drove straight through without stopping.[^c25] He explained later:

> "I think they told me to come and I didn't come. Then I told them I was coming, and they weren't ready. So I had to go through!"[^c26]

The Donington pit lane cut off the final corner, so that lap, a 1:18.029, became the fastest of the race.[^c25] Sources count Senna's pit visits differently: Motorsport.com has four stops plus that drive-through, while Motor Sport has five stops, the last a precautionary switch to wets. Prost stopped seven times.[^c27] Hill stopped six times.[^c44] He summed up his race like this:

> "It was like a nightmare! The worst conditions you ever wanted to race in."[^c35]

## The finish, and "change cars with me"

Senna won by 1:23.199 from Hill. Everyone else he lapped.[^c28] Prost came home third, ahead of Johnny Herbert's Lotus, Riccardo Patrese's Benetton and Fabrizio Barbazza's Minardi.[^c29] Barrichello ran in the top four for a long time but retired a few laps from the end with a fuel pressure problem.[^c30]

It was Senna's 38th Formula 1 win.[^c31] After three rounds he led the championship with 26 points to Prost's 14.[^c32] Ten years earlier, Donington was where Senna had first driven a Formula 1 car, and it had been a Williams.[^c36]

[[media:m3]]

He described the race simply:

> "Conditions like this is gambling, and it's taking chances that pays off. I think we gambled good and we got the result."[^c49]

And on the pit stops he added:

> "I don't know how many times we stopped for tyres, it was surely the record in any race for pitstops."[^c34]

At the press conference Prost went through his list of problems at length: the gearbox, the stalled engine, the wrong tyre pressures.[^c45] Senna, sitting beside him, replied with one line:

> "Perhaps you should change cars with me?"[^c33]

## Why it is called the greatest lap

"The greatest lap" is not our verdict. It is what people who saw the race say.

Nigel Roebuck wrote in his Autosport race report:

> "This was Senna at his most majestic."[^c37]

Motor Sport's race report in May 1993 called Donington "quite possibly the best drive of Senna's career".[^c40] Four years later Shaun Campbell concluded in the same magazine that Senna had won the race on the first lap.[^c41]

Thirty years after the race, Bishop wrote that in his view it was the best opening lap in Formula 1 history.[^c38] He also quoted Emerson Fittipaldi, who watched on television at home in Miami:

> "The first lap? Pure genius. The next day I call him. 'Ayrton, that lap is impossible,' I tell him."[^c39]
