## A team without a single podium

Toro Rosso arrived at Monza as Red Bull's junior team. It was created in 2006 out of Minardi, which Red Bull had bought, and the base stayed where it was — in Faenza.[^c2] Minardi itself had entered Formula 1 in 1985[^c3] and in 340 Grands Prix had scored no win, no podium and no pole.[^c4] The team was still small: fewer than 170 people worked in Faenza, and Vettel himself compared those resources with BMW, McLaren and Ferrari — not in his own team's favour.[^c34]

Sebastian Vettel had turned 21 on 3 July.[^c5] He had already made his mark in Formula 1: he made his debut at the 2007 United States Grand Prix for BMW Sauber, finished eighth and, at 19, became the youngest points scorer in history.[^c40] That same summer, from the Hungarian Grand Prix, he replaced Scott Speed at Toro Rosso.[^c41] He was due to move to Red Bull the following season, so Monza was one of his last races for Toro Rosso.[^c35] Nobody expected it to become the one that mattered most.

## Saturday: a pole nobody predicted

Rain fell at Monza on Friday, on Saturday and on Sunday.[^c6] Qualifying turned into a lottery, and the favourites did not win it. Lewis Hamilton switched early to full wet tyres and missed the moment when the track was at its fastest.[^c44] He failed to reach the final part of qualifying and started 15th — the furthest back he had ever started in Formula 1.[^c9]

At Toro Rosso, by contrast, everything came together. After a difficult Friday the team changed the set-up overnight, and Vettel finally felt at one with the car.[^c42] He recalled: "I was joking with my engineers, we were saying if it is wet, then we have to go for pole position."[^c43] That is exactly what happened: in the final session on 13 September he lapped in 1:37.555 and took the first pole both for himself and for the team.[^c7] Heikki Kovalainen in the McLaren was 0.076 seconds slower.[^c8] Vettel's team-mate Sébastien Bourdais qualified fourth, so both cars from Faenza were in the first two rows.[^c10]

"I never dreamt of being on pole," Vettel said at the press conference.[^c13] He was blunt about the conditions: "It was nearly impossible to see anything."[^c14] He became the youngest pole-sitter in Formula 1 history[^c11] — the record had previously belonged to Fernando Alonso.[^c12] And speaking to AFP he added: "Now everyone will know there are two Italian teams in Formula One."[^c15]

## Sunday: 49 laps in front

The race on 14 September[^c1] started behind the safety car because the track was flooded. The safety car pulled into the pit lane after two laps.[^c16] Bourdais's Sunday was over before it began: he stalled at the start and immediately lost a lap.[^c17] The Frenchman reached the finish still a lap down.[^c17]

[[media:m2]]

Vettel, on the other hand, drove as if wet Monza were his home circuit. He led until his first pit stop, dropped to fourth for a few laps after it, and when his rivals came into the pits he went back to first and never gave it up.[^c18] His second stop was to fit intermediate tyres: the track was gradually drying.[^c20] According to the lap-by-lap data, Vettel was in front for 49 of the 53 laps.[^c19] After his final stop he talked himself through the plan on the radio: "I am fuelled to finish, there is nothing, no pitstops, so just keep it together."[^c45]

He explained the main reason for his success simply: "The safety car helped me and being first and having no visibility problems at all was the key."[^c24]

At the flag Vettel was 12.512 seconds ahead of Kovalainen, with Robert Kubica third for BMW Sauber.[^c21] For the Pole it was a second podium at Monza — he had taken his first here in 2006.[^c46] Felipe Massa finished sixth and Hamilton seventh, and the Briton kept his championship lead by a single point.[^c22] On that day the title fight took a back seat.

"I think for sure this is the best day of my life," Vettel said after the race.[^c23] In the team's press release he called it "a fantastic race, a fantastic weekend with pole as well and a fantastic strategy this afternoon."[^c53] Even Ron Dennis, the McLaren boss whose driver had been beaten, conceded: "he deserved to win."[^c48]

## Records of a single day

Vettel became the youngest Grand Prix winner in history. His exact age differs between reports: counting from his date of birth to race day gives 21 years and 73 days, but some publications at the time wrote 74 days.[^c25] The previous record belonged to Alonso — 22 years and 26 days at the 2003 Hungarian Grand Prix.[^c26] For Germany it was the first win since Michael Schumacher retired in 2006.[^c27]

The Vettel — Kovalainen — Kubica podium was the youngest in history: the trio's average age was 23 years, 11 months and 16 days.[^c30]

For the team, Monza ticked every box at once: first pole, first podium and first win in a single weekend.[^c28] One more detail: for the first time a Grand Prix was won by a Ferrari-engined car whose chassis was not a Ferrari.[^c29] And in a single Sunday the team scored more points than in its first two seasons as Toro Rosso.[^c50]

[[media:m3]]

Vettel himself was relaxed about the numbers: "For me it's not that important, rewriting history or statistics."[^c52]

## The people from Faenza

On the podium with the team stood Gerhard Berger — Toro Rosso's co-owner, who had himself won at Monza in 1988 driving a Ferrari, exactly 20 years earlier.[^c31] The team's technical director was Giorgio Ascanelli — in 1988 he had been Berger's race engineer.[^c51] Berger did not hide his surprise: "I really underestimated the potential of this boy."[^c47] Franz Tost, then the team principal,[^c36] gave all the credit to the driver in the team's press release: "This was Vettel's race weekend."[^c32] A few days later, in an interview with Autosport, he admitted: "I didn't expect that in 2008 we would win here in Monza."[^c33] And he summed it up briefly: "It was a perfect drive. From the start to the finish."[^c49]

## Twelve years on — the same circuit

The second win only came on 6 September 2020: the team, by then called AlphaTauri, won again at Monza, this time with Pierre Gasly.[^c37] Tost, who had been at Monza in 2008, was running AlphaTauri by 2020.[^c38]

That race was stopped by a red flag, and leader Lewis Hamilton was penalised for entering a closed pit lane.[^c39] It has its own story: "Monza 2020: Gasly, a red flag and a closed pit lane".
