## Before Suzuka

In 1989 Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna drove for the same team, McLaren-Honda. The title counted each driver's best 11 results from 16 races.[^c7] Prost scored 81 points that season, but only 76 counted.[^c8]

Because of that rule a third place was already worth nothing to Prost: any new score would only replace his worst counted result, the 4 points for third in Jerez. Senna had 60 and nothing to drop. Two wins at 9 points each would take him to 78, two more than Prost.[^c9]

There was no trust left between the teammates. According to Prost, before the race at Imola they agreed not to attack each other before the first corner, and Senna broke that at the restart. Senna said there was no agreement.[^c10] In July Prost announced he was leaving McLaren,[^c11] and at Monza, during the Italian Grand Prix weekend, it was confirmed that his next team would be Ferrari.[^c12]

## The race

Senna took pole with 1:38.041,[^c13] Prost was second, 1.730 s slower.[^c14] Prost made the better start and led.[^c15] By lap 11 he was 4.9 s clear; each driver stopped once for tyres, Prost on lap 21, Senna on lap 23.[^c16]

After the stops Senna started to close in. Sources differ on how close he was by lap 40: 0.42 s or about a second.[^c17]

## The chicane

On lap 47, seven laps from the end, Senna went for the pass into the chicane before the pit straight. Prost completed 46 laps, which is why some sources say «lap 46».[^c18] Senna dived down the inside, Prost turned in to the apex, the cars locked together, both engines stalled and the McLarens stopped at the entrance to the escape road.[^c19]

> «This time I said before the race that if this happens I would not open the door.»[^c20]
> — Alain Prost · after the race, 22.10.1989

Prost climbed out straight away. Marshals pushed Senna's McLaren, he bump-started the engine and rejoined through the escape road, past the chicane.[^c21] Whether that push was legal is still argued: the rules allowed it if the car was stopped in a dangerous place.[^c22]

Senna drove nearly a full lap with a damaged nose, pitted for a new one and rejoined about 5 s behind Alessandro Nannini's Benetton.[^c23] He passed Nannini at the same chicane on lap 51 (lap 50 in another account)[^c24] and took the chequered flag first. He was not allowed onto the podium.[^c25]

> «Somebody who was there closed the door. I was the moral winner.»[^c26]
> — Ayrton Senna · after the race, 22.10.1989

## The ruling

The stewards disqualified Senna for missing the chicane: he rejoined the track somewhere other than where he left it.[^c27] The push itself was not penalised. According to Joe Saward, the version of the decision that reached the Court of Appeal was a different one, citing Article 63 of the Code on safety.[^c28]

The win went to Nannini, the first and only one of his career. Riccardo Patrese was second and Thierry Boutsen third, both in Williams cars.[^c29]

Sources disagree on whether FISA president Jean-Marie Balestre was in the stewards' room. RaceFans writes that he «joined the stewards». 8W Forix cites two books: according to Heinz Prüller, FISA officials insisted he was not with them; according to Terry Lovell, he came in but took no part in the decision.[^c30]

## Appeal and winter

McLaren appealed the same day. The case went to the FIA International Court of Appeal in Paris, which sat on Friday 27 October.[^c31] The court did more than uphold the disqualification: it fined Senna $100,000 and gave him a six-month suspended ban, citing earlier offences.[^c32]

> «We went there expecting to defend a case of shop-lifting, and we were faced defending car theft, shoplifting, rape, murder and pillage.»[^c33]
> — Ron Dennis, McLaren team boss · on the Court of Appeal hearing, November 1989

## A year later

Prost was the 1989 champion, his third title,[^c40] and moved to Ferrari. On 21 October 1990 they met at Suzuka again, now in different teams. Senna started from pole, Prost second. Both went out at the first corner of the first lap after a collision, and the title went to Senna.[^c41]

A year after that, at Suzuka on 20 October 1991, Senna said he had decided beforehand: if Prost got the better start, he would not give way at the first corner.[^c42]

> «If tomorrow Prost beats me off the line, at the first corner I will go for it, and he better not turn in.»[^c43]
> — Ayrton Senna on Suzuka 1990 · Suzuka, 20.10.1991
