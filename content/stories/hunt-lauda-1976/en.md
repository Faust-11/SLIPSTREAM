## A champion going for a second title

Niki Lauda began 1976 as the reigning champion: a season earlier he had given Ferrari its first title since 1964.[^c1] At first the new championship looked like more of the same. Of the first six races, the Austrian won four: Brazil, South Africa, Belgium and Monaco.[^c2]

James Hunt and McLaren, meanwhile, were collecting wins and stewards' decisions in equal measure. In Spain he finished first, but after the race his car was found to be wider than the rules allowed, by 1.8cm according to Goodwood, and the win was taken away.[^c3] McLaren argued that the tyres had simply expanded during the race, and appealed.[^c41] The win was only restored in July, and the team was fined $3,000.[^c3][^c41]

## Brands Hatch: the win that vanished

At the British Grand Prix at Brands Hatch the race fell apart at the very first corner. The two Ferraris touched, Hunt's McLaren was launched into the air, and the race was stopped with a red flag.[^c4] While officials argued over whether drivers could take the restart in spare cars, the McLaren mechanics repaired Hunt's race car. He started again and ran second for a long time, until Lauda began to have trouble selecting gears. On lap 45 Hunt passed him and went on to win.[^c4]

Protests against Hunt being allowed to take the restart at all were rejected, but Ferrari appealed.[^c5] The verdict came only shortly before the Canadian Grand Prix: Hunt was disqualified and the Brands Hatch win was handed to Lauda.[^c6] By then, though, the whole season had been turned upside down.

## Nürburgring, 1 August

The German Grand Prix on 1 August 1976 was held on the old Nordschleife.[^c7] The drivers were unhappy about safety at the circuit, but they raced.[^c8] Hunt took pole, Lauda started second.[^c42] On the second lap, at Bergwerk, Lauda lost control of his Ferrari 312T2. The car went through the fencing, hit an earth bank and bounced back onto the track in flames.[^c9]

Harald Ertl and Brett Lunger could not avoid it. They, Guy Edwards and Arturo Merzario, who stopped his own car nearby, rushed to pull Lauda out of the fire.[^c10] According to Motor Sport, Merzario reached into the flames to release the seat belt buckle.[^c11]

Lauda had suffered burns and inhaled toxic fumes; he was taken to the University Clinic in Mannheim.[^c12] For the next few days he fought for his life.[^c43] His condition was such that he was given the last rites.[^c13] Then came a dramatic improvement, and within a week Lauda was having skin grafts.[^c44]

The race, meanwhile, was restarted. Chris Amon decided he had had enough and did not take the restart.[^c45] Hunt won.[^c14] It was Formula 1's last race on the Nordschleife: in March 1977, after a track inspection, the drivers' representatives decided that no further top-flight races would be held there.[^c15]

## Six weeks

Lauda missed the races in Austria and the Netherlands. In that time Hunt finished fourth in Austria and won at Zandvoort.[^c16] Ferrari, meanwhile, hired Carlos Reutemann, and at Monza the team ran three cars.[^c17]

[[media:m3]]

Because at Monza, six weeks after the crash, Lauda came back.[^c18] The first day of practice was hard. "It was like a pilot reacting to every air pocket. Not the way you should feel," he recalled.[^c19] Yet he qualified fifth and finished the race fourth. Ronnie Peterson won in a March.[^c20][^c47] Jackie Stewart said: "It was the most courageous thing I have ever witnessed in sport."[^c21]

Monza went badly for Hunt. After qualifying, McLaren's fuel was found to exceed the octane limit, and his times were struck out.[^c22] Because rain had spoiled Friday's session, Hunt, Jochen Mass and John Watson had technically not qualified at all. They were allowed to start because those who had failed to qualify had already left and Guy Edwards agreed to stand down.[^c46] Hunt started from the back of the grid and spun off on lap 12.[^c22]

Hunt answered in North America, winning the Canadian and United States Grands Prix.[^c23] Going into the finale, Lauda led him by just three points, 68 to 65.[^c24]

## Fuji: deluge and decision

The last round of the season was held on 24 October 1976 at Fuji.[^c25] Mario Andretti took pole for Lotus, Hunt started second and Lauda third.[^c26]

On race day it poured, and fog settled over the circuit.[^c27] The start was delayed, by about an hour and a half in total.[^c28] There was a long argument over whether to start at all: some drivers were against it, but the race went ahead.[^c48] Hunt took the lead straight away. After the second lap Lauda pulled into the pits and climbed out of his car.[^c29] "It's just like murder to be racing out there. I'm not going to do it," Autosport reported him saying.[^c30] He was not the only one: Emerson Fittipaldi, Carlos Pace and Larry Perkins also withdrew of their own accord.[^c31] Lauda later made no secret of it: he simply did not want to carry on in those conditions.[^c49]

Hunt stayed in front for a long time. On lap 22 Vittorio Brambilla even challenged for the lead, but spun. Jochen Mass ran second for a while, giving McLaren a one-two, but crashed on lap 36.[^c53] The track, meanwhile, was drying, the rain tyres were wearing out, and Hunt began to fade. On lap 62 he was passed by Patrick Depailler and Andretti.[^c50] Then Hunt's left front tyre gave way. He pitted, the mechanics changed all four wheels in 27 seconds, and he rejoined in fifth.[^c32]

He spent the closing laps attacking, passed his rivals and finished third, a lap down. Andretti won the race.[^c33] "I was determined to not do anything stupid today," the winner said.[^c54] Hunt finished convinced he had lost the title.[^c34] He later explained: "I was absolutely determined not to think I was World Champion and then be disappointed."[^c35] When he drove into the pits, team boss Mayer kept shouting at him: "James, you've won!" Hunt could not hear, and it took two or three minutes for it to sink in.[^c51]

## One point

Third place gave Hunt four points, and with them the title: 69 for Hunt, 68 for Lauda.[^c36] Of the rival who had taken himself out of the race, the new champion said: "I think Niki made absolutely the right decision."[^c37]

Ferrari took the constructors' championship.[^c38] Over the season Hunt won six races, Lauda five.[^c39] And the very next year, 1977, Lauda was world champion again.[^c40] He won his third title in 1984, beating Alain Prost by half a point.[^c52]
