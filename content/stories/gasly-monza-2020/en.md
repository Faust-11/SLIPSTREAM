## A Faenza team and a demoted driver

In September 2020 the team from Faenza arrived at Monza under a new name. Toro Rosso, which had grown out of Minardi, was racing as AlphaTauri from that season — named after Red Bull's fashion brand.[^c34] The base stayed where it was, in Faenza.[^c35] Before then the team had won a Grand Prix only once[^c36] — in 2008, at this very circuit, when Sebastian Vettel won from pole in the rain.[^c44] That race has its own story: "Monza 2008: rain, Toro Rosso and a 21-year-old Vettel".

Pierre Gasly was 24.[^c41] His career had already taken a sharp turn. In 2019 Red Bull promoted him to its senior team, but on 12 August it announced that Alexander Albon would take his seat from the Belgian Grand Prix and Gasly would return to Toro Rosso.[^c30] He was sent back after 12 races,[^c31] with 63 points to Max Verstappen's 181.[^c32] Back at Toro Rosso, Gasly scored his first podium late that season, at the Brazilian Grand Prix.[^c33] After Monza he summed up that period like this: "I've been through so much in the space of 18 months."[^c48]

## A race turned upside down in one lap

The Italian Grand Prix on 6 September 2020 was round eight of the season.[^c1] Lewis Hamilton started from pole and led the race.[^c2] Gasly started tenth.[^c3] The day began badly for the home crowd's team: Vettel, now a Ferrari driver, retired on lap six with a brake failure.[^c4]

AlphaTauri called Gasly in for his pit stop on lap 19.[^c5] A lap later Kevin Magnussen's Haas stopped at the exit of the Parabolica and the safety car came out — wiping out Hamilton's lead of almost 13 seconds over Sainz.[^c6] To recover the car safely, race control closed the pit lane: according to the stewards' document, at 15:41:47 the message appeared on the timing screen and the pit-lane-closed lights came on at the same time.[^c7]

Hamilton entered the pit lane at 15:41:59 — 12 seconds after it closed.[^c8] Mercedes fitted him with medium tyres.[^c54] Antonio Giovinazzi followed him in with the Alfa Romeo, at 15:42:54.[^c9] Both received a 10-second stop-and-go penalty for breaching Article 28.14 of the Sporting Regulations; the stewards noted specifically that this was a mandatory penalty.[^c10] Hamilton was also given 2 penalty points on his superlicence, taking him to 8 in 12 months.[^c61]

"I take responsibility for that," Hamilton said afterwards.[^c40] He explained that he had been watching the safety car delta on his display and did not see the boards showing the pit lane was closed.[^c46]

## Red flag

Shortly after the race resumed, Charles Leclerc lost his car at the Parabolica on lap 24 and hit the barrier. He was unhurt.[^c11] The barrier had to be repaired, so the race was stopped with a red flag.[^c12] The stoppage lasted 25 minutes.[^c13] During that time Hamilton went to see the stewards, and they showed him onboard video of the pit-lane-closed boards.[^c45]

On the grid for the restart Hamilton was first, Lance Stroll second and Gasly third.[^c59] Stroll had not pitted at all before then, so the red flag effectively handed him a free pit stop: his tyres were changed during the stoppage.[^c60] The race restarted from a standing start on lap 28 of 53.[^c14] Hamilton got away first but served his penalty at the end of that lap[^c16] and rejoined last, having lost about 30 seconds.[^c17] AlphaTauri had put Gasly on medium tyres, while Stroll could not bring his up to temperature quickly, and Gasly passed him under braking into the first chicane.[^c15][^c55] Tost later admitted: "We had good luck with the red flag."[^c38]

## 26 laps under pressure

From lap 28 to the flag Gasly never gave up the lead.[^c18] But it was anything but calm. Carlos Sainz fought his way up to second in the McLaren and started to close the gap: with ten laps to go there was less than three seconds between them.[^c19] "I want this win," the Spaniard told his engineer on the radio.[^c20]

According to Gasly, over the last five laps "my tyres were completely gone".[^c52] He said after the finish that he "almost shunted 10 times".[^c24]

Gasly won by 0.415 seconds from Sainz. Stroll finished third for Racing Point, 3.358 seconds behind the winner.[^c21] Hamilton, dropped to the back by his penalty, came home seventh and set the fastest lap of the race — 1:22.746.[^c22] Sainz said after the race that without the red flag he could have won himself.[^c47] Even so, second place was the best result of his career,[^c51] and Stroll took the second podium of his Formula 1 career.[^c49] The best of the Mercedes, Ferrari and Red Bull drivers was Valtteri Bottas — in fifth.[^c50] Gasly's AlphaTauri team-mate Daniil Kvyat finished ninth, so both cars from Faenza scored points.[^c53]

## What the win meant

"It's unbelievable," Gasly said after the finish.[^c23] About the team he said: "They gave me my first opportunity in F1, they gave me my first podium."[^c25] And he added about himself: "I'm not someone who gives up on anything, I always fought for everything in my life until I made it to F1."[^c56]

[[media:m3]]

The numbers were big. Gasly became the 109th Grand Prix winner in the history of the world championship[^c27] and the first French winner since Olivier Panis won in Monaco in 1996.[^c26] For the first time since the 2013 Australian Grand Prix, when Kimi Räikkönen won for Lotus, a race was won by a team that was neither Mercedes, Ferrari nor Red Bull.[^c28] And Formula 1 had last seen a podium without a single driver from those three teams at the 2012 Hungarian Grand Prix.[^c29]

## Monza again, Faenza again

For the team it was a second win — and the first since Vettel at Monza in 2008.[^c36] AlphaTauri team principal Franz Tost[^c43] praised his driver: "Pierre drove a fantastic race without any mistakes."[^c57] And he drew the parallel himself: "Twelve years after the great victory in Monza in 2008 with Sebastian Vettel…"[^c37]

The next day there were celebrations in Faenza too. The team's founder Giancarlo Minardi told the ANSA agency: "Yesterday there were parades of thrilled fans in Faenza and around the works."[^c39] In his words, there was also "a touch of pride with respect to Ferrari"[^c58] — at Ferrari's home race, both of its cars had retired.[^c42]

Twice in 12 years the team from Faenza won the Italian Grand Prix. Both times at Monza, both times as an outsider: in 2008 the rain helped, in 2020 a red flag and a closed pit lane.[^c36][^c44]
