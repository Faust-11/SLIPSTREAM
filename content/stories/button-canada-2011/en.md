## Rain at the start

On 12 June 2011 Formula 1 arrived in Montreal for round seven of the season: 70 laps of the Circuit Gilles Villeneuve.[^c1] Sebastian Vettel took pole for Red Bull, Fernando Alonso's Ferrari started alongside him, and Jenson Button's McLaren was only seventh.[^c2]

It was raining. The FIA decided to start the race behind the safety car, so everyone fitted wet tyres, and the field spent the first four laps following it.[^c3] For Pirelli it was the first wet race since the company became Formula 1's tyre supplier, and almost nobody had real experience of its wet-weather rubber.[^c4]

[[media:m2]]

## A team-mate in the wall

As soon as the safety car pulled in, Lewis Hamilton clipped Mark Webber's Red Bull at Turn 2 and spun him, dropping the Australian to 14th. "I think Lewis thought the chequered flag was at Turn 3...", Webber said afterwards.[^c37] Soon Michael Schumacher nudged Hamilton wide at the hairpin, and Button moved up to sixth.[^c38]

Hamilton wanted the place back. He got a better exit from the final chicane and tried to pass Button on the pit straight. The cars touched and Hamilton was out, having completed only seven laps.[^c5] Button explained later:

> "I couldn't see anything when he was alongside me. I couldn't see anything in my mirrors."[^c6]

He apologised to his team-mate.[^c7] Button himself pitted straight away for intermediates, his first visit, on lap eight.[^c8]

Then came the penalties. Button was given a drive-through for speeding behind the safety car, and he served it on lap 13.[^c9] The gamble on intermediates didn't pay off either: the rain got heavier, and on lap 19 Button was back in the pits for new wets.[^c10]

## Two hours under the red flag

Then a proper downpour hit Montreal. The race was red-flagged on lap 25 according to most sources, or on lap 24 according to Pirelli and one Motorsport.com writer.[^c11] Spectators waited about two hours for the restart.[^c12]

[[media:m3]]

The red flag also reshuffled the strategies. Those still on their first set of wets got fresh ones during the stoppage for free.[^c13] Button had already changed his on lap 19 and paid for it with time in the pit lane.[^c10] Alonso was not among the lucky ones. "We had no luck at all," he said.[^c39]

## A Ferrari on the kerb, and last place

On lap 35 Button made his fourth stop, for used intermediates.[^c14] Two laps later he attacked Alonso at the first chicane. They touched wheels, the Ferrari spun, beached itself on the kerb, and Alonso was out.[^c15]

The McLaren was hurt too. Button damaged his car and on lap 37 made his fifth stop, under the safety car triggered by that very incident.[^c16] After it he was 21st, which meant last.[^c17] There were 30 laps to go.[^c18] By Autosport's count, he was more than a minute and 40 seconds behind the leader, Vettel, at that point.[^c19]

But Button stayed on the lead lap, and the safety car bunched the field back up. Grandprix.com later wrote that if Alonso's Ferrari had not been stuck on the kerb, Button could not have won the race.[^c40]

## The chase

The safety car came out again and again, including for the stranded Ferrari and Nick Heidfeld's crash, and each period compressed the field.[^c20] By Autosport's estimate, those periods saved Button more than two minutes in total.[^c41] On lap 51 he pitted for the sixth and final time, for supersoft dry tyres.[^c21]

[[media:m4]]

In all, Button went down the pit lane six times: five pit stops and one drive-through, 2 minutes 21 seconds in total.[^c22] Five stops plus a drive-through is why some reports say five pit stops and others say six.[^c22] For comparison, Vitaly Petrov, who finished fifth, stopped twice and spent 47 seconds in the pit lane. "It felt like I spent more time in the pits than on the pit straight," Button said.[^c42]

With 15 laps to go he was already fourth, behind Vettel, Schumacher and Webber.[^c43] Then Heidfeld, after contact with Kamui Kobayashi, hit the wall, and the safety car came out once more. That left a 10-lap sprint.[^c23] By lap 65 Button had passed Webber and Schumacher and was second, about three seconds behind Vettel.[^c24] On the penultimate lap he set the fastest lap of the race, a 1:16.956.[^c25]

## Half of the final lap

Vettel had led the race almost from the start.[^c26] After the last restart he held a gap of about four seconds and did not push flat out. "I was probably a bit too cautious there," he admitted later.[^c44]

On the final lap Vettel wanted to pull far enough away that Button could not use DRS on him. He pushed too hard, put a wheel on the wet part of the track, and the rear stepped out.[^c27] After the race Vettel took the blame:

> "It is clearly down to me. I locked up the rear a little bit."[^c28]

Button later summed it up: "In a 70-lap race, I led half a lap."[^c29] Without DRS, he admitted, he would not have had a chance.[^c45]

He finished 2.709 seconds ahead of Vettel. Webber was third and Schumacher fourth.[^c30] Fourth place disappointed Schumacher: since his return to Formula 1 he had yet to stand on the podium.[^c46]

## The longest race in history

From the start to the winner crossing the line, 4 hours 4 minutes 39.537 seconds passed.[^c31] It is the longest race in Formula 1 history.[^c32]

After Montreal the rules changed: from 2012 a race was limited to four hours including stoppages, and from 2021 to three.[^c33] That is why Mercedes calls it a record that can no longer be beaten because of the FIA regulations.[^c34] RaceFans worked out that under today's rules the race would have ended after 31 laps instead of 70, and Button would have been tenth.[^c36] The same site calls the race one of the best in Formula 1 history.[^c47] Paul Hembery of Pirelli said straight after the race that it was "one of the most thrilling and unpredictable grands prix we have seen so far this season".[^c48]

At the press conference Button said: "It has been a very emotional three hours or however long it has been since the start." He called the win "possibly my best".[^c35]
