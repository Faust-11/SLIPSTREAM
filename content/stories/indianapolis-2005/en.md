## Six cars on the grid

On 19 June 2005 Formula 1 held the ninth round of the season at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway.[^c1] Twenty cars completed the formation lap, but only six lined up for the start: fourteen drivers on Michelin tyres turned into the pit lane and never came back out.[^c15] Seven of the ten teams refused to race.[^c16] What remained were two Ferraris, two Jordans and two Minardis, all on Bridgestones.[^c19]

To understand how it came to this, we have to go back two days.

## Friday: two Toyota crashes

On Friday 17 June, Toyota's reserve driver Ricardo Zonta spun off at Turn 5 after feeling a problem at the rear of the car.[^c2] A few minutes later his team-mate Ralf Schumacher crashed. His Toyota went off at the final corner, the banked turn onto the pit straight, in the same place where he had had a big accident a year earlier.[^c3][^c4] "I was approaching the last corner when I felt something go wrong on the left-hand side," Ralf said.[^c3] In both cases the team suspected the tyres.[^c2]

Ralf was taken to hospital for checks after complaining of blurred vision.[^c5] In qualifying and the race the second Toyota was driven by Zonta.[^c5]

## Saturday: the letter that changed everything

On 18 June the heads of Michelin's F1 programme, Pierre Dupasquier and Nick Shorrock, wrote to FIA race director Charlie Whiting: they had been unable to identify the root cause of the failures and had no total assurance that the qualifying tyres could last the race.[^c6] The problem lay in the 2005 rules: the race had to be run on the tyres a car had qualified on.[^c6] Michelin put it plainly: "the race must be performed with the qualifying tyres."[^c6]

[[media:m2]]

Meanwhile, Jarno Trulli put his Toyota on pole.[^c14] That evening Michelin flew 26 tyres to its research base in Akron, Ohio; by Sunday morning the trip had turned up nothing new.[^c33]

## Sunday: talks hit a wall

The Michelin teams wanted a temporary chicane before Turn 13 to slow the cars through it.[^c8] Whiting replied in a letter on 19 June. "We are very surprised that this difficulty has arisen," he wrote.[^c7] The FIA offered alternatives: fit different tyres and accept a penalty, or change wheels during the race if safety demanded it.[^c7] The letter ruled out a chicane: it would break the rules and be unfair to the teams that had brought suitable tyres.[^c7]

In a statement after the race the FIA explained its refusal: a chicane would have meant changing the circuit without following modern safety procedures.[^c8] The federation said it had offered another route, monitoring speeds in Turn 13 and penalising any excess, but the Michelin teams would only accept if the Bridgestone cars were slowed as well.[^c9] The drivers later said in a joint statement that such a speed restriction "would have been impossible to comply with and impossible to police."[^c10]

FIA president Max Mosley made it clear that a race with a chicane would not count as a round of the world championship.[^c11] Ferrari did not back the chicane.[^c12] Tyre barriers were already being moved from Turn 10 to build one. Whiting warned that the circuit would then lose its homologation and there would be no race at all. The barriers were put back.[^c34] Jordan and Minardi initially sided with the Michelin teams but raced in the end. Minardi owner Paul Stoddart explained that he sent his cars out only because Jordan had broken the agreement.[^c13]

## A race that was booed

After the formation lap fourteen cars peeled off into the pits, pole-sitter Trulli among them.[^c15] The six Bridgestone runners were left on the grid.[^c15]

Sources disagree on the size of the crowd: Reuters reported 120,000 spectators, History.com about 100,000.[^c17] They agree on the reaction: loud booing, and bottles and beer cans flying onto the track.[^c18] Track president Joie Chitwood said afterwards he could not recall anything like it at any Indianapolis event.[^c24]

"I am sorry, but we didn't have a choice," said Williams driver Nick Heidfeld.[^c23]

Michael Schumacher won, with Rubens Barrichello second.[^c19] A lap down came Tiago Monteiro and Narain Karthikeyan (Jordan), and two laps down Christijan Albers and Patrick Friesacher (Minardi).[^c19] It was Schumacher's only win of 2005.[^c20] On the podium a subdued Schumacher skipped the champagne spray into the crowd.[^c22]

Monteiro, who started 17th, took third place: his first and, as his career turned out, only F1 podium.[^c21] He celebrated with his mechanics and the Jordan fans in yellow. "This was our moment," the Portuguese recalled.[^c32]

## Who was to blame

The two sides immediately started blaming each other. The FIA pointed out that on 1 June it had already written to teams and tyre makers that tyres should be built to be reliable under all circumstances.[^c35] Michelin replied that it had proposed solutions, including a chicane, that it considered safe, and that the sporting authority had rejected them.[^c36]

[[media:m4]]

Michelin was not a competitor in the championship, so the FIA could only go after the teams.[^c26] On 29 June the World Motor Sport Council found the seven guilty on two counts: failing to ensure they had suitable tyres (though the council accepted "strong mitigating circumstances"), and wrongfully refusing to start when they had the right to use the pit lane on every lap.[^c26] Three other charges were dropped, including refusing to race under a speed restriction.[^c26] Any penalty was deferred to a meeting on 14 September.[^c26]

It never got that far. On 14 July in Monaco the FIA Senate examined new evidence submitted by six of the teams and accepted that they had been contractually bound to follow their tyre supplier's instructions.[^c27] The Senate recommended cancelling the verdict.[^c27] On 22 July the council voted overwhelmingly to agree, and the guilty verdicts against the seven teams were cancelled.[^c28]

Michelin, meanwhile, tried to make amends with the public. On 28 June the company promised to refund tickets for the race and to buy 20,000 tickets for the 2006 US Grand Prix for those who had been in the stands.[^c25] Michelin stressed it was not legally obliged to do so.[^c25] A fans' class action against FOM, Michelin and the Speedway was dismissed by a federal judge in June 2006.[^c29]

## What it cost F1 in America

That same evening Chitwood said the Speedway was "monumentally disappointed" and would evaluate its involvement with F1.[^c24] The Indianapolis race survived two more seasons, but on 12 July 2007 the Speedway announced it would not be on the 2008 calendar, after eight consecutive Grands Prix since 2000.[^c30] Speedway boss Tony George said he and Bernie Ecclestone were "unable to agree how to keep Formula One in Indianapolis"; GrandPrix.com reported that the sanctioning fee had become too high for the track.[^c30]

The next US Grand Prix was not held until November 2012, at a new circuit in Austin.[^c31] The country waited five years.[^c31]
